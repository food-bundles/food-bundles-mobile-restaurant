Design system, navigation & screen inventory

A B2B procurement tool for restaurants, hotels and their staff to source fresh produce from FoodBundles in Rwanda. The system below is tuned to read trustworthy and efficient — restrained color, dense-but-legible type, and one deliberate signature: how order status is visualized. Please approve the tokens and nav shape before I design any screens.

Step 1 · Design tokens

Color

Anchored on a deep agricultural green — fresh produce, growth and the "land of a thousand hills" — paired with a warm marigold lifted from market produce (maize, ripe fruit) that carries CTAs and the "acting now" status. Deliberately not the flagged AI defaults: no cream-and-terracotta serif, no near-black-plus-neon, no broadsheet hairlines. For a procurement tool, restraint is the aesthetic; distinctiveness comes from the type and the status signature, not an exotic hue.

Step 1 · Design tokens

Typography

Two faces. Space Grotesk for headings and — critically — every price: its engineered, even-width figures scan cleanly down a column. IBM Plex Sans for body and UI: enterprise-grade legibility that signals "tool, not toy." Prices always use tabular figures and thousands separators.

Step 1 · Signature element

The Order Status Rail

The one thing the app is recognized by. Every order lives on a connected-dot rail mapping the exact flow — Pending → Confirmed → Preparing → Ready → In transit → Delivered. Completed steps fill Leaf; the current step pulses Marigold; upcoming steps stay hollow. It appears three ways, always named consistently: OrderStatusRail (detail), OrderProgressTrack (list cards), OrderStatusBadge (label).

Step 1 · System pattern

Every screen ships three states

Loading, empty and error are designed once as reusable treatments, not per screen. Skeletons mirror the final content shape; empty and error states speak in the interface's voice.

Step 2 · Navigation architecture

Five tabs, five stacks, full-focus flows

A persistent 5-item bottom tab bar. Each tab owns its own stack; deep-focus flows (checkout, verification, auth) take over the full screen and hide the bar.

Role context
Restaurant · Hotel · Affiliator each see one role-relevant price per product. Affiliators act under a restaurant — a persistent "Ordering as [Restaurant]" banner sits above the tab bar in their session. Data is revalidated on focus so prices are never stale.
